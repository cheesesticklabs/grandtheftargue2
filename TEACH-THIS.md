# AI Dilemma Club — teacher guide (nine-debate edition)

The site includes the original three fictional thought experiments **plus six new cases**. It is designed to let students take a position, justify it with a story detail, hear a competing view, reveal a twist, and reconsider. **You do not need to teach all six at once.** Choose any three for a roughly 30-minute period, or use all nine across three classes. All nine are available from the navigation.

Spaces and capitalization do not matter. Each password unlocks that section and all preceding sections, so you **can skip directly to a chosen debate**. To finish early, give students `six` and have them use the Exit Ticket navigation button. The last password also unlocks Save & Turn In.

If students have an old edition's local browser data, they keep their existing answers, but passwords and twist reveals reset for the expanded lesson. Students' responses save locally in their own browser; **they are not submitted automatically**. Exports include only cases students actually answered or voted on, so a three-debate lesson does not produce three empty case sections.

## Suggested 30-minute class

- 3 minutes: Do Now, written independently.
- 7 minutes each: three teacher-selected dilemmas. Students vote and write before discussion. Reveal the twist only after an initial response.
- 4 minutes: Exit Ticket. Students defend a position and answer an objection.
- 2 minutes: Download PDF (or text) and turn it in via your class system.

For a focused AI literacy lesson, choose cases **7, 8, and 9** (for higher-stakes ethical arguments), or **4, 5, and 6** (for AI literacy review). Students can use the vocabulary *Chinese Room*, *synthetic media*, and *AI-washing* as appropriate. There is no single correct philosophical vote. Evaluate reasoning, relevant details, and the willingness to address objections.

### Notes on accuracy

All events and characters are fictional. The AI referee's accuracy percentages are invented for its scenario. The "AI best friend" rulebook is a thought-experiment device, **not** an account of how all real language models work. Also, do **not** teach that a product using if/then rules cannot be AI; rule-based expert systems are part of AI history. The AI-washing issue in case 6 is the product's specifically false claim to learn about each individual student.

## Hosting

Upload the **contents of the `letsargue-main` folder**, not the outer ZIP folder itself, to any static host (GitHub Pages, Netlify, etc.) with `index.html` at the published root. The `jspdf.umd.min.js` file is bundled so the PDF download works without fetching a library from the internet.
